How it works

Talk. Let go. It's written.

Cado sits in the menu bar and stays out of the way until you ask for it.

  1. 01

    Hold to talk

    Hold ⌃⇧ in any app. Cado listens while you speak.

  2. 02

    Let go

    Your speech is transcribed on your Mac, using a model picked for your hardware.

  3. 03

    Text lands

    Words appear at the cursor, with punctuation, and your clipboard is put back.

Dictation

Type with your voice, anywhere you can type.

Browsers, native apps, the Terminal and the Claude Code CLI. Press ⌃⌥Space to start and again to stop for longer passages. Esc cancels.

Meetings

Notes that say who said what.

Cado records your mic as Me and only the meeting app, such as Zoom, Teams or Meet in a browser, as Others. Stop, and a Markdown note lands in your notes folder.

Meeting notes require macOS 14.2 or later.

  • Ask firstCado offers to record when a meeting starts. It never starts by itself.
  • SummariesKey points, decisions and action items, written on your Mac with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26.
  • Your folderPoint it at an Obsidian vault and notes appear there.

Private by design

Your voice stays on your Mac.

On-device by default. No Cado account. Here is exactly when the app uses the network.

Stays on your Mac by default

  • Your audioMeeting audio is held in memory and discarded once the note is written.
  • What you say, as textTranscripts stay in your notes folder unless you choose cloud summaries.
  • Your settings

What does use the network

  • Model downloadsFrom Hugging Face during setup or when you click Download. Files are checked against a pinned checksum. Apple Speech models are managed by macOS.
  • Update checkOn GitHub, about once a day. Turn it off in Settings.
  • Anonymous launch countOff by default. Never audio, text or file names.
  • Cloud summaries, only if you opt inChoosing OpenRouter and confirming sends meeting text to OpenRouter and your chosen model provider. Never audio. Apple Intelligence stays the default.

First launch: what Cado asks for

A short setup window walks you through permissions and offers a speech model for your Mac.

Microphone

So it can hear you. macOS asks the first time you dictate.

Accessibility

So the hotkey works and Cado can type into other apps. Turn Cado on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.

Screen & System Audio Recording

macOS asks the first time you record a meeting. Dictation never needs it.
