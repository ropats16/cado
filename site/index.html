Private dictation and meeting notes, on your Mac.

Hold a key, talk, and your words appear in any app. Record a meeting and get a Markdown note of who said what. It all runs on your Mac. Nothing goes to the cloud.

Two jobs, done on your Mac.

Dictate anywhere with a hotkey

Hold ⌃⇧, speak, let go. Your words are typed at the cursor, with punctuation, in browsers, native apps, the Terminal and the Claude Code CLI. For longer passages press ⌃⌥Space to start and again to stop. Esc cancels.

  1. Hold Cado listens
  2. Release It transcribes
  3. Done Text lands

Meeting notes with Me and Them

Click Record Meeting. Cado records your mic as Me and only the meeting app (Zoom, Teams, Meet in a browser and more) as Them. Stop, and a note with the conversation as Me/Them turns lands in your notes folder.

Meeting notes need macOS 14.2.

Everything on-device

Speech recognition runs on your Mac with whisper.cpp, on the GPU on Apple Silicon. Intel Macs work too, just slower. Cado picks a model that fits your chip and RAM.

Notes are plain Markdown

Meeting notes are ordinary .md files in a folder you choose, ~/Documents/Cado by default. Point it at an Obsidian vault and they appear there.

Your voice stays on your Mac.

Never leaves your Mac

  • Your audio
  • What you say, as text
  • Your settings
  • Meeting recordings, held in memory and thrown away once the note is written

What does use the network

  • Model downloads from huggingface.co, when you run setup or click Download. Each file is checked against a pinned checksum.
  • Update check on GitHub, about once a day. Turn it off in Settings › Updates.
  • Anonymous launch count, off by default. Never audio, text or file names.

Download the DMG

  1. Download Cado-<version>.dmg from the latest release.
  2. Open it and drag Cado to Applications.
  3. Open Cado. Look for the avocado in your menu bar.

brew install --cask ropats16/tap/cado

If Homebrew says the tap is untrusted, run brew trust ropats16/tap first.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 or later (macOS 14.2 for meeting notes)
  • Apple Silicon or Intel
  • About 1 GB free for the default model, less on smaller Macs

First launch: what Cado asks for

Cado opens a short setup window that walks you through these and offers a speech model for your Mac.

Microphone

So it can hear you. macOS asks the first time you dictate. Click Allow.

Accessibility

So the hotkey works and Cado can type into other apps. Turn Cado on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.

Screen & System Audio Recording

Only for meeting notes. macOS asks the first time you click Record Meeting. Dictation never needs it.
